Help & Terminology

Plain-language explanations of how the NJIT Thesis Auditor works and what its results mean.

Important disclaimer.

This tool is an automated research prototype that checks PDF dissertation files for common formatting problems. It is not an official NJIT approval system. A result of no problems found, or any combination of results, does not mean your dissertation has been approved by the Graduate Studies office. Every result is advisory — please verify each flagged item against the official NJIT formatting guidelines and consult your Graduate Studies coordinator.

The tool is not perfect: it may miss some real issues, and it may sometimes flag content that is actually correct. Human review is always required.

How it works

  1. Upload a thesis or dissertation PDF — or audit a public PDF URL, or try a built-in sample.
  2. The tool runs a series of automated formatting checks against common NJIT requirements: margins, spacing, fonts, page numbering, headings, table of contents, and similar layout details.
  3. The results page lists the possible formatting problems found, organized by topic and linked to the page where each was found.
  4. You review each item and verify it manually against the official NJIT thesis/dissertation formatting guidelines.

This tool does not read or judge the content of your dissertation — only structural and layout details. No dissertation body text, titles, author names, or captions are shown in the web UI.

How results are organized

After the audit finishes, the results page shows either "No problems found" or "Problems found", followed by a list of the possible formatting problems detected. Similar problems that repeat across many pages are grouped into a single card so the list stays readable.

Every item is a signal to review, not a confirmed error. The tool can sometimes flag things that are actually fine, and it can also miss real problems. Always check each item against the official NJIT formatting guidelines before deciding whether to make a change.

"No problems found" does not mean your dissertation has been approved. It only means the checks that ran did not detect anything to flag. Many formatting and content requirements cannot be checked automatically at all, so always submit your dissertation through the official NJIT Graduate Studies process and respond to any feedback from your coordinator.

Not checked / not reliably checkable

Some requirements cannot be checked automatically, or a check may not run because:

  • Missing or unreliable text layer. If the PDF was scanned or generated from an image, the text layer may be absent and text-based checks may not work correctly.
  • Section not detected. If the tool cannot locate a specific section (such as the Abstract or Table of Contents), checks that rely on that section will produce no findings.
  • Requires manual judgment. Some requirements (such as whether content is appropriate or consistent with your research) cannot be assessed automatically.
  • Experimental or disabled check. Some checks are turned off by default because they are not yet reliable. They appear in the Rule Reference but are marked experimental.

Glossary of terms

Term Meaning
Rule ID (P013) An internal identifier for a specific check. Each rule has a stable ID like P013 that links to its full description in the Rule Reference. Rule IDs appear as clickable links in your audit results.
Category A grouping label for related checks, such as "PDF page numbering" or "Margins / overflow". Categories help you find all findings related to the same topic.
Repeated-problem summary
(Grouped problems)		 When the same formatting issue appears on many pages, the tool combines them into a single card and lists all affected pages. This avoids an overwhelming list of nearly identical findings. The card shows occurrence count and a compact page range (e.g., 3–7, 10, 14–16). Expand the card to see individual occurrences.
Individual finding
(Raw finding)		 A single finding before grouping. The "All individual findings" view shows every finding separately, including those combined in the grouped view. Summary totals always reflect the raw finding count.
Occurrence count How many times the same issue was detected across your document.
Affected page The PDF page number where the issue was found. Page 1 is the first page of your document as the PDF numbers it.
High-volume triage Some checks (such as margin and font checks) can produce hundreds of findings in a typical dissertation. These findings are presented in a compact summary panel at the top of the results so you can quickly understand the overall pattern before looking at individual findings. The raw findings are still available below.
Experimental check A check that is still being validated and is turned off by default. Results from experimental checks are advisory only.
Rules flagged The number of distinct checks that produced at least one finding. A higher number means more different types of issues were detected.

Why Graduate Studies review is still required

The NJIT Thesis Auditor automates only a subset of formatting checks. Many important requirements fall outside its scope:

  • Content requirements — whether your abstract, introduction, conclusions, and references meet academic and departmental standards.
  • Typographic consistency — fine-grained font, spacing, and alignment details that require visual inspection.
  • Committee and signature page accuracy — names, titles, and dates that require human verification.
  • Citation style compliance — whether citations follow the required style (APA, Chicago, etc.).
  • Figure and table quality — image resolution, label accuracy, and placement that require visual judgment.
  • Accessibility requirements — whether the PDF meets accessibility standards.
  • Departmental variations — formatting requirements that differ by department or program.

After running this tool, review its findings as a checklist of items to inspect, then consult the official NJIT Thesis and Dissertation Guidelines and your Graduate Studies coordinator before final submission.

The tool does not communicate with NJIT systems and cannot submit or approve your dissertation.
