Help & Terminology

Plain-language explanations of what the NJIT Thesis Auditor reports and what its results mean.

Important disclaimer.

This tool is an automated research prototype that applies heuristic checks to PDF and LaTeX dissertation files. It is not an official NJIT approval system. A result of PASS, no findings, or any combination of results does not mean your dissertation has been approved by the Graduate Studies office. All findings are advisory signals — please verify every flagged item against the official NJIT formatting guidelines and consult your Graduate Studies coordinator.

Because many checks are heuristic (based on pattern-matching rather than exact rules), the tool may miss some issues and may sometimes flag content that is actually correct. Human review is always required.

Audit outcomes

FAIL Error — likely correction required

The tool found a likely formatting or structural problem that normally requires correction. For example, a duplicate \label in your TeX source, or a page number appearing where it should not be.

Because some checks are heuristic, please review the cited page or source location before making changes. A FAIL finding is a strong signal but not a guarantee of an actual error.

WARNING Warning — review recommended

The tool found something unusual or potentially inconsistent that should be reviewed. A warning is not always a confirmed violation — it may reflect a formatting pattern that is less common or may require manual judgment to assess.

Warnings often arise from heuristic checks on visual layout where the tool cannot be certain. Review each warning against NJIT guidelines to decide whether it requires a change.

PASS Check passed

A particular automated check completed without detecting the problem it was designed to find. This means the specific pattern or condition the check looks for was not observed.

PASS does not mean your complete dissertation has been approved. See What PASS does not mean below.

No findings

None of the checks that ran produced a FAIL, WARNING, or PASS finding. This could mean no issues were detected, or that the relevant checks did not apply to your file (for example, if a required section could not be located).

Zero findings is encouraging but does not prove complete compliance, because some requirements may not be machine-checkable and some sections may not have been reached reliably.

What PASS does not mean

A PASS result — or a report with zero findings — means only that the specific automated checks that ran did not detect the patterns they were designed to detect. It does not mean:

  • Your entire dissertation meets NJIT formatting requirements.
  • Graduate Studies has reviewed or approved your submission.
  • Every NJIT rule has been verified — many requirements are not machine-checkable at all.
  • The checks ran successfully on every section of your document.

Always submit your dissertation through the official NJIT Graduate Studies process and respond to any feedback from your coordinator.

Not checked / not reliably checkable

Some requirements cannot be checked automatically, or a check may not run because:

  • Required input unavailable. A rule that needs a compiled PDF will not run on a TeX source upload, and vice versa.
  • Missing or unreliable text layer. If the PDF was scanned or generated from an image, the text layer may be absent and text-based checks may not work correctly.
  • Section not detected. If the tool cannot locate a specific section (such as the Abstract or Table of Contents), checks that rely on that section will produce no findings — not a PASS.
  • Rule requires manual judgment. Some requirements (such as whether content is appropriate or consistent with your research) cannot be assessed automatically.
  • Experimental or disabled rule. Some rules are disabled by default because they are not yet stable. They appear in the Rule Reference but are marked experimental.

Glossary of terms

Term Meaning
Rule ID (P013) An internal identifier for a specific check. Each rule has a stable ID like P013 that links to its full description in the Rule Reference. Rule IDs appear as clickable links in your audit results.
Category A grouping label for related checks, such as "PDF page numbering" or "Cross-references". Categories help you find all findings related to the same topic.
Repeated-problem summary
(Grouped problems)		 When the same formatting issue appears on many pages, the tool combines them into a single card and lists all affected pages. This avoids an overwhelming list of nearly identical findings. The card shows occurrence count and a compact page range (e.g., 3–7, 10, 14–16). Expand the card to see individual occurrences.
Individual finding
(Raw finding)		 A single finding before grouping. The "All individual findings" view shows every finding separately, including those combined in the grouped view. Summary card totals always reflect the raw finding count.
Occurrence count How many times the same issue was detected across your document.
Affected page The PDF page number where the issue was found. Page 1 is the first page of your document as the PDF numbers it.
Source location For TeX source audits, the file name and line number in your .tex source where the issue was detected (for example, main.tex:42).
High-volume triage Some rules (such as margin and font checks) can produce hundreds of findings in a typical dissertation. These findings are presented in a compact summary panel at the top of the results so you can quickly understand the overall pattern before looking at individual findings. The raw findings are still available below.
Confidence An internal score (low / medium / high) indicating how certain the tool is about a particular finding. Lower-confidence findings are more likely to be false positives and should be reviewed carefully.
Triage tier A classification within a high-volume group that helps prioritize which occurrences to review first (for example, a dominant pattern shared by most pages versus an outlier page).
Heuristic check A check that uses pattern-matching or statistical inference rather than an exact rule. Heuristic checks are useful but may occasionally produce false positives or miss actual issues.
Experimental check A rule that is still being validated and is disabled by default. It can be enabled manually with special options. Results from experimental checks are advisory only.
Highest severity The most serious severity level present in the audit results. If any FAIL findings exist, the highest severity is FAIL; if only warnings, it is WARNING; if only passes, it is PASS. This gives a quick top-level signal about your document.
Rules flagged The number of distinct check rules that produced at least one finding. A higher number means more different types of issues were detected.

PDF audit vs TeX source audit

PDF layout audit (P011–P082)

Runs when you upload a compiled .pdf file. Checks visual layout properties: margins, page numbers, spacing, font usage, table of contents formatting, and similar layout requirements. These checks analyze the rendered output of your document.

TeX source audit (P001–P010)

Runs when you upload a .tex or .zip file containing LaTeX source. Checks source-level issues: duplicate labels, invalid cross-references, risky formatting commands, and document structure. The source is not compiled — the tool reads the source files directly.

A PDF audit and a TeX source audit are complementary — they check different aspects of your dissertation and neither alone is sufficient.

Why Graduate Studies review is still required

The NJIT Thesis Auditor automates only a subset of formatting checks. Many important requirements fall outside its scope:

  • Content requirements — whether your abstract, introduction, conclusions, and references meet academic and departmental standards.
  • Typographic consistency — fine-grained font, spacing, and alignment details that require visual inspection.
  • Committee and signature page accuracy — names, titles, and dates that require human verification.
  • Citation style compliance — whether citations follow the required style (APA, Chicago, etc.).
  • Figure and table quality — image resolution, label accuracy, and placement that require visual judgment.
  • Accessibility requirements — whether the PDF meets accessibility standards.
  • Departmental variations — formatting requirements that differ by department or program.

After running this tool, review its findings as a checklist of items to inspect, then consult the official NJIT Thesis and Dissertation Guidelines and your Graduate Studies coordinator before final submission.

The tool does not communicate with NJIT systems and cannot submit or approve your dissertation.
